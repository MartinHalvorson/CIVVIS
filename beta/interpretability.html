      How it is counted

      A step is sorted by what its name says it does: one named claim_… asks for a city’s queue, one named …_purchase spends. Many stand behind a condition and do nothing on most turns. A square is a call, not a size: advanced_units, one square near the end, moves every unit on the map.

              How it is counted

              One tournament batch of self-play: six seats a game, a Continents map, Online speed, 250 turns. A seat aims for one of the six victories, either told which or left to choose. Six seats share a game, so an even share of the wins is one in six.

              Bottom up: one rule at a time

              A gene is a switch on one rule, such as a Settler always has somewhere to go. Its price: the win rate of the seats with it on, less those with it off.

              Every rule, by what it is worth

              A dot is a gene. Right: seats win more with the rule on. Higher: surer that it helps.

                How it is counted

                The difference is in percentage points of win rate, over every seat that priced the gene. A gene is kept on or turned off by its last three batches, with a margin so that one batch cannot flip it, so a rule in the best genome can stand left of zero here and one outside it to the right. A rule that costs wins stays in the pool, switched off, as the record of what was tried. In a tournament every seat draws its own genes: one of the best genome is on for about three seats in four, any other for about one in four.
