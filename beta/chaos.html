          How it is counted

          Every seed is played once at each frequency by the same CIVVIS AI agents on every seat, so the worlds at one seed differ in their disasters only. 1x is Civilization VI as shipped at standard disaster intensity; the game’s own top setting, hyperreal, is 2.75x. The disasters are the game’s own nine kinds with their own damage; only how often they strike changes.

          A figure a game is the mean over the games at that frequency. A world is read against its calm self on the last tenth turn both games reached, because a game ends when somebody wins and the two seldom end together: the share is the total over the chaos games divided by the total over the same seeds at 1x. A line turn by turn is the mean over the games still being played, and stops when half are over. A civilization has lost half its people when its population falls to half its own earlier peak or less. The agents do not know the dial was turned.

          “Who comes through” splits the civilizations at 100x and over into two halves by one difference at a time, within a frequency, and compares each half’s people with their calm selves’. Only differences settled before the damage are compared: the land and the empire a start grows in the calm world, and what a civilization had on turn 50. The same split at 10x and 30x, where the storm does no harm, is the control: a leader falls back in any replay. A difference is drawn only if it holds when the worlds are drawn again 400 times, whole worlds at a time; one that does not says nothing, however wide it stands. It shows what went together in these games, not what caused what. All of it is one game’s rules and one set of agents: how this system gives way under shocks, not a forecast of ours.
