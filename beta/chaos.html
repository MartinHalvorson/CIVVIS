      How it is counted

      Every seed is played once at each frequency by the same CIVVIS AI agents on every seat, so the worlds at one seed differ in their disasters only. 1x is Civilization VI as shipped at standard disaster intensity; the game’s own top setting, hyperreal, is 2.75x. The disasters are the game’s own nine kinds with their own damage; only how often they strike changes. The agents do not know the dial was turned.

      A world is read against its calm self on the last tenth turn both games reached, because a game ends when somebody wins and the two seldom end together: a share is the total over the chaos games divided by the total over the same seeds at 1x. Working land is improved land not lying pillaged. A game is won outright when somebody wins before the turn limit, not on score at it.

      Every figure under chaos is tested against the same worlds played calm, with an exact paired test (Wilcoxon’s signed ranks; for won or not, the sign test). It counts only when a difference that big would come by luck less than once in 1,000 times: with 40 figures tested, the chance that even one that counts is luck stays under 1 in 20. Its range is where 95 in 100 of 10,000 redraws of the worlds put it, whole worlds at a time. A figure that does not count is drawn hollow, and no lesson rests on it.
