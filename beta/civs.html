    How it is counted

    CIVVIS’s capital’s build order: what it worked on, in order. A step is a unit, building, district, wonder or project the capital’s production finished, or one it put production into and dropped unfinished, marked “dropped”: a Scout started on turn 1 and dropped at pop 2 for a Settler reads “Scout, dropped”, then “Settler”, so the first step is always one the capital could start on turn 1. The first steps are listed best win rate first; press one to list the steps after it, then the third, eight steps deep. A row is CIVVIS’s win rate over the games whose build order opened with exactly the steps down to it and, in brackets, those games. An item bought with Gold or Faith, or handed over (a pantheon’s free Settler or Builder), is not a step. Every finished game against the Deity AI on the run ledger counts, in a tournament or not: a win is a CIVVIS victory; a loss is a rival’s victory, our elimination, or a game conceded early. A build order only one game played is not opened further.

    By turn 100 the map shows which victory is in reach.

    Victory aimed for

    Win rate (games). A win of any kind counts.

    How it is counted

    Against the AI, a game counts once however many runs it took: a win is a CIVVIS victory; a loss is a rival’s victory, our elimination, or a game conceded early. A city is one CIVVIS founded, not one it took, on the turn it went down. A religion’s founder and follower beliefs are the two chosen when it was founded; the beliefs added when it is enhanced are not counted. A game is first invaded on the first turn another civilization’s unit fights CIVVIS on its own ground: it attacks one of our cities or districts, or one of our units while it or its attacker stands on our land. A barbarian’s or a city-state’s attack does not count, and neither does a fight past our borders. Never means no civ invaded us before the game ended, however early that was. Cities at turn 50 and science at turn 100 are read on the first board of that turn, off the run’s summary: CIVVIS’s own, and the most any rival it had met held. A game that ended before the turn, or whose summary does not hold it, is not in that chart. A column says what went with winning, not what made the win: a game that is going well also founds more cities and keeps up in science. In self-play every seat is a CIVVIS AI agent and one in six wins, so a seat’s par is 16.7%; a self-play seat records its cities and whether it founded a religion, not its pantheon, beliefs, invasions, marks at turns 50 and 100 or the victory it aimed for, and its cities from 2026-11-20 on. A figure over fewer than ten games is faint.
