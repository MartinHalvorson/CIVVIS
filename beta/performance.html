Experimental is the processor the benchmarks were run on. Theoretical is a calculation from two scores, not a run.

How it is worked out

One game on one core is the measured time, times the measured processor’s single thread score, over the row’s own. Games a minute on every core are the measured run’s, times the row’s multithread score, over the measured processor’s. A score is not this simulator, so a theoretical row is what the scores predict until the benchmark is run on that processor. The age is how old the readings are that a row’s figures rest on.

How it is measured

  1. Human Player (me) One person plays one game of Civilization VI. Some are faster, some are slower. I’m a bit on the slower side but 5h seems reasonable.
  2. CIVVIS → Firaxis computer control Civilization VI itself, Firaxis’s game, with CIVVIS at the controls of one seat.
  3. Firaxis self-play Civilization VI itself with Firaxis’s own AI in every seat.
  4. CIVVIS self-play One simulator game on one core, every seat a CIVVIS AI agent.
  5. Parallel games, M5 Max, all 18 cores CIVVIS self-play on every core of one M5 Max at once: the time from one finished game to the next.
  6. Civ 6 Computational Irreducibility One game on one core with nothing left in it that the game does not need. A guess: nobody knows where this is. a guess, self-play
  7. M5 Max, Theoretical Max Civ 6 Throughput The most games one M5 Max could finish: the irreducible game on every core at once. A guess on a guess. a guess, irreducibility
  8. Reasonable Local Hardware Scale-up Theoretical Hardware of our own, scaled up within reason past the one M5 Max. Theoretical: a calculation, nothing has run on it. the M5 Max
  9. Distributed RL fleet Many machines playing self-play at once for reinforcement learning. A potential: no such fleet exists. potential, up to
  10. Theoretical A million games in the time a person plays one.

Amortized: many games play at once, and the time is from one finished game to the next.

        How it is counted

        A time is one game’s wall-clock seconds, start to victory, in the simulator with nothing drawn. Seven games are played at once on one MacBook Pro (M5 Max) that is doing other work too, so a time moves with the machine’s load as well as with the code. In the chart of batches the darker band holds eight games in ten, and the lighter band all but the fastest and the slowest one in a hundred. Its scale goes by multiples, so the same drop anywhere on it is the same share of the time saved.
