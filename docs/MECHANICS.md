# Civ 6 mechanics coverage

Tracked first against the official [Gathering Storm Civilopedia](https://www.civilopedia.net/en-US/gathering-storm/concepts/intro/),
with secondary references used only where the in-game documentation omits a
numeric rule.
Status: ✅ implemented · 🟡 intentionally scoped relative to the full commercial ruleset.

*Numbers* are now tracked against a stronger authority than either: the rules
database the game itself ships. `python tools/civ6_fidelity.py` diffs
`data/*.json` against it and currently reports zero unwaived divergences
across twenty-seven tables — units, technologies, civics, buildings,
districts, adjacency, boosts, goody huts, eras, great people, global
parameters, map sizes, great-work values, happiness bands, WMDs, routes,
terrains, features, resources, improvements, tech-gated improvement
upgrades, wonders, policies, governments, beliefs, promotions and
projects. Run it after any
data change, and see
[FIDELITY.md](FIDELITY.md) for what the audit covers next.

| Per-civilization event stream | ✅ | the engine records what happened to each civilization — cities founded/lost, wars and peace, technologies and civics completed, wonders raised, Great People recruited, era transitions, victory — in Unciv's categorized-notification shape, with a tile to look at where there is one. | War ledger | ✅ | every declaration carries one engine-kept conflict id even when teams, defensive allies, or an Emergency open several combat fronts. | War and peace duration | ✅ | a war runs the shipped `DIPLOMACY_WAR_MIN_TURNS` before either side may settle it, and the peace then binds both for `DIPLOMACY_PEACE_MIN_TURNS` — ten turns each on standard speed, scaled by game speed — with no casus belli reopening it early. A Suzerain's treaty binds its city-states. Neither action is offered to an agent while it is illegal. Before this the AIs declared and unmade wars on consecutive turns, and one quarrel between the same two civilizations was recorded — correctly — as a dozen one-turn wars; `civvis audit` now fails a game where either rule is broken |
| War weariness | 🟡 | prolonged war now costs Amenities in **every** city a civilization owns, not only the ones fighting: the shipped `WAR_WEARINESS_POINTS_FOR_AMENITY_LOSS` of 400 points buys one negative Amenity, so weariness feeds the ordinary Happiness bands and their yield, growth and revolt consequences. Fighting accrues `WAR_WEARINESS_PER_COMBAT_IN_FOREIGN_LANDS` 2 in territory that is neither yours nor an ally's and `_IN_ALLIED_LANDS` 1 otherwise, reduced by the policy and government modifiers `war_weariness_multiplier` already computed but nothing had consumed; `WAR_WEARINESS_PER_WMD_LAUNCHED` charges 10 for a nuclear launch. It decays `_DECAY_TURN_AT_WAR` 50 per turn while any war runs, `_AT_PEACE` 200 once they are all over, and `_DECAY_PEACE_DECLARED` 2000 outright for both signatories of a treaty. 🟡 Two shipped inputs are not modelled and would only ever raise the total: the `_PER_UNIT_KILLED` 3 charged when your own units die (the engine has no combat-death choke point distinct from the 41 other reasons a unit leaves play), and the era and no-Casus-Belli scaling the Civilopedia describes without publishing its factors. The three `_LOSS_OVER_REQ_AMENITIES_*` caps are likewise unmodelled, their semantics being unclear from the parameter names alone |

| Hex map, fog of war, terrain/features/resources | ✅ | all six stock Civ VI map-size profiles and Pangaea, Continents, Small Continents, Inland Sea, and Lakes topology scripts, plus **Planet**, which is not a cylinder at all: a geodesic globe of hexagons and exactly twelve pentagons (the same icosahedral construction as Uber's H3), holding within a few percent of the tiles its size's rectangle would, with adjacency, distance and latitude taken from the sphere and the twelve pentagons kept under water so every land tile still has six neighbours; connected rivers follow shared hex edges. **Rivers are traced downstream by the shipped `AddRivers`/`DoRiver` method**: a headwater is sought in the highlands, at the shipped `RIVER_SOURCE_RANGE_DEFAULT` of 4 from any water that is already fresh and `RIVER_SEA_WATER_RANGE_DEFAULT` of 3 from the sea, over Civ VI's four passes; the channel then walks the corner graph downhill by `GetRiverValueAtPlot` — the tile's own elevation weighted twenty to one against its six neighbours, deserts drawing the flow and the map edge repelling it — at the stock eleven-twelfths discount for holding a straight course, and stops the moment it reaches water. So **every river has exactly one mouth**, and a trace that reaches no water is discarded rather than drawn, which is what keeps the map free of stubs and spurs. Two terms the Lua does not need are added back because our terrain keeps only the four elevation rungs and not the fractal height field they were cut from: an inland slope, without which a greedy flow on flat ground is a random walk that wanders hundreds of tiles before blundering into the sea, and a valley sunk under each finished river, without which nothing ever drains into anything and the map is all trunks and no tributaries. A fifth pass of our own then seeds tributaries within reach of an existing river and keeps only those that reach it, so systems branch at genuine confluences; total density is held to the shipped `RIVER_PLOTS_PER_EDGE` of one river edge per twelve land tiles of each landmass, a fifth of it reserved for tributaries. Lakes are generated by the shipped `AddLakes` pass in its stock position — after the rivers, so none of them is dammed — against the shipped `LAKE_PLOT_RANDOM` of 40, on inland plots only: a lake is water the sea cannot reach, which is a precondition of placing one rather than a property checked afterwards. Every enclosed body is then sorted by area at the shipped `LAKE_MAX_AREA_SIZE` of 9, which is the whole distinction between the two kinds of inland water — at most nine tiles is a Lake, carrying fresh water, Fisheries and the site Huey Teocalli must be built on, and anything larger is an inland sea of Coast that ships work and no city drinks from. The Lakes script inverts the usual world: land nearly everywhere, with the basins left by the fractal, rather than an ocean, as the water between civilizations. **Generation follows the shipped map scripts' method rather than per-tile rolls**: cylindrical fractal fields (`src/fractal.rs`) cut at percentiles, as `TerrainGenerator.lua` and `MountainsCliffs.lua` do — tectonic plates woven into the mountain field give ranges along collision lines with their own foothills, mountain passes and the stock nine-in-ten demotion of peaks that reach the sea; Desert is the driest quarter of its own field inside the subtropics and Plains the wetter half of another, so both arrive as regions; latitude band edges are roughened by a variation field; coastlines are cut out of a fractal, so they carry bays, capes and offshore islets; the continental shelf is one tile of Coast plus the stock's three expansion passes at a quarter chance each, so shallow water runs from one tile in open sea to five or more in a bay; and Woods/Rainforest/Marsh/Reef use the shipped adjacency weights and running share caps (18%/40% of the tropical band/3%/9%), so vegetation grows in stands. `cargo run --release --bin mapdump` renders a world and reports these shares. The rules model Woods, Rainforest, Marsh, all Floodplains variants, Oasis, Reef, Geothermal Fissure, polar Ice, Volcano, Volcanic Soil, Impact Zone, and burning/burnt vegetation states. Generation places reefs in Coast, ice in polar water, and volcanic/fissure features around tectonic relief. Cinematic view has distinct terrain cutouts plus grouped one-to-four-tile Natural Wonder landmarks and climate/disaster overlays; strategic view uses flat colors and compact symbols. Multi-landmass scripts distribute major starts across playable components. Current sight is the unit's stock range as a **hard cap** — elevation decides what a unit sees *past*, never how far — resolved against the shipped `SightModifier`/`SightThroughModifier` levels: Mountain 2, Hills 1, flat 0, with Woods and Rainforest adding 1 to whatever they stand on and each Natural Wonder carrying its own (Everest and Yosemite 2, Uluru and Pamukkale 1, Crater Lake/Pantanal/Dead Sea/Great Barrier Reef 0). Cover hides everything no taller than itself, so a Mountain shows over Woods and bare Hills but not over a wooded Hill, and a unit's own vantage is its terrain alone — standing in Woods is not standing higher. On top of that sit sight promotions (Sentry sees through vegetation but not rock), aircraft vision, City Center/Encampment vantage, and unconditional empire borders plus their one-tile outer ring. Suzerains see the exact three-tile radius around their city-state; level-2 Military Alliances and active Emergency coalitions share live sight and newest map memory. Explored tiles and cities retain serialized last-seen state under fog, while units, targetable attacks, and barbarian alerts use only current visibility. A resource that waits on a technology or civic — every strategic deposit, Antiquity Sites and Shipwrecks — waits for the **spectator** too: the omniscient view names it once the first major civilization has the research to recognise it, and never before, so an Ancient world opens without an Iron or Niter symbol anywhere on it. Tournament Civ VI hands its spectator every deposit from turn one; CIVVIS deliberately shows the world as the players know it instead. A seated player is unaffected — its own research still gates its own map Major civilizations are placed at the shipped `START_DISTANCE_MAJOR_CIVILIZATION` of 12 with `START_DISTANCE_RANGE_MAJOR` 2, so starts sit 10-14 tiles apart rather than as far apart as the landmass allows; on the tournament lobby's Standard map with eight majors 96% of nearest-neighbour distances land in that band, none below it |

## Trade and Quick Deals

Press **D** in a human game to open Quick Deals. Sell, Buy, Luxury, and
Strategic filters compare every living major's current offer in one list,
sorted by the human player's net value. Each card exposes the exact terms and
both civilizations' positive equivalent-Gold gain; accepting revalidates the
same terms atomically, so a stale treasury, resource export, or declaration of
war cannot force a deal through.

Lump Gold, Diplomatic Favor, strategic-resource stockpile quantities, Great
Works, and captured-Spy releases transfer immediately. Gold per turn, usable Luxury access (including Amenity
allocation), and directional Open Borders last 30 Standard-speed turns. War terminates those
ongoing agreements and restores exported Luxury access, but does not reverse
an already completed strategic transfer. The engine rejects gifts and any
custom economic exchange for which either side's modeled gain is not positive.
AI civilizations periodically choose at most one of those same mutually
favorable offers.

Borders remain open to everyone until their owner completes Early Empire.
After enforcement begins, every ordinary unit step and every future segment of
an AI route requires directional Open Borders, a current Alliance, or war.
Both parties need Early Empire before an Open Borders grant can be traded.
Traders, religious units, Rock Bands, and Terracotta Army Archaeologists retain
their stock exceptions; Suzerain, Gunboat Diplomacy, and Portugal access applies
to city-states. Expired access invalidates cached routes and safely expels units
that no longer have permission.

Reference basis: the in-game [Trade, Demand, and Discuss Civilopedia entry](https://www.civilopedia.net/en-US/standard-rules/concepts/diplo_7/),
the directional [Open Borders Civilopedia entry](https://www.civilopedia.net/en-US/gathering-storm/concepts/diplo_9/),
and the compare-all-offers workflow of the [Quick Deals mod](https://steamcommunity.com/sharedfiles/filedetails/?id=2460661464).

## Combat and zone of control

Zone of control does **not** unlock through a technology or civic; it applies
from turn 1. The Military Tradition civic unlocks the separate +2-per-unit
flanking and support bonuses.

Every melee-capable land line in the roster exerts ZOC, including Scout,
cavalry, anti-cavalry, and Giant Death Robot units. Ranged and bombard land
units do not, unless a promotion such as Suppression grants it. Every naval
surface unit exerts ZOC, including naval ranged ships, Privateers, and Aircraft
Carriers; Submarines and Nuclear Submarines are the exceptions. City Centers,
the Encampment family, and Oppidums project into every adjacent land or water
tile until pillaged. Unit ZOC stays in the provider's native domain and cannot
cross a river, while defensible-district ZOC crosses rivers.

Cavalry (including ranged cavalry), Naval Raiders, and air units ignore
incoming ZOC. A linked civilian or support unit inherits that immunity from
its escort. Military and religious units that enter ZOC keep unused Movement
and may still attack, pillage, spread religion, or promote, but cannot move to
another tile that turn—even with move-after-attack. Civilian and support units
lose all remaining Movement and receive no follow-up actions. A unit that
begins its turn inside ZOC may leave as its first action, but cannot attack
first and then leave while the ZOC remains.

Religious units of different civilizations and different religions exert ZOC
against one another regardless of war; units of the same religion never do.
Defensible districts affect foreign religious units only while their owners
are at war.

Reference basis: the in-game [Civilopedia Zone of Control entry](https://www.civilopedia.net/en-US/standard-rules/concepts/movement_3/),
the detailed [Civilization VI ZOC rules](https://civilization.fandom.com/wiki/Zone_of_control_%28Civ6%29),
and the general hex-grid [zone-of-control definition](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zone_of_control).

## Nuclear weapons

A device is built, not trained: the `build_nuclear_device` and
`build_thermonuclear_device` projects add to a national stockpile rather than
placing a unit, and each stockpiled device charges its shipped Gold maintenance
every turn it sits there.

Delivery is a platform question. A launch order names a city, and the shot is
carried by whichever platform stands closest to the target: the city center
itself, any of that city's unpillaged **Missile Silos**, or any **Nuclear
Submarine** the launching civilization has at sea. The device's own
`icbm_strike_range` from the shipped WMDs table — 12 for a nuclear device, 15
for a thermonuclear one — is measured from the platform that fires, which is
what makes an SSBN worth its cost: it carries the range with it. The target
tile must be revealed, and a blast that would touch a major or city-state the
launcher is at peace with is not a legal order.

Detonation covers the shipped blast radius (1 and 2). Ground zero kills a
full-health unit outright and levels a city's Outer Defenses; the outer rings
wound severely and halve what walls remain. A struck city loses half its
population, and improvements, districts and their non-immune buildings inside
the radius are pillaged. Every tile in the radius carries fallout for the
device's shipped duration.

Fallout is not decoration. A tile under it yields nothing, and any unit
beginning a turn on it takes damage and cannot heal there, so a blast denies
ground for as long as it lasts rather than only for the turn it lands.

Diplomatically, a strike is the single most expensive thing a war can contain:
Grievances against every civilization whose land it touched, and the largest
single addition to the launcher's own War Weariness in the game.

Every detonation is recorded on the game as a strike record and announced to
the launcher, every victim, and every other living major — nobody misses a
mushroom cloud. Clients read the same records to place the blast on the map and
to mark the moment in the war log.

Reference basis: the [Nuclear device](https://civilization.fandom.com/wiki/Nuclear_device_%28Civ6%29)
and [Thermonuclear device](https://civilization.fandom.com/wiki/Thermonuclear_device_%28Civ6%29)
pages, and the shipped `WMDs` table this implementation reads its ranges,
radii and fallout durations from.
