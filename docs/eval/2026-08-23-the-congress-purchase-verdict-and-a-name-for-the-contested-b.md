# The congress purchase verdict, and a name for the contested board

_2026-08-23 · `agent/mbp-m5-max-128/claude-fable-denial`_

## What was asked

Three questions, all downstream of the same hole. Of **299** terminal live
games, **107 were lost to a rival's victory before the clock — diplomatic 58,
culture 38**, religious 5, technology 5, conquest 1 — **and 15 of those while
our own score was the highest on the board** (`docs/EVAL_STATUS.md`, as
regenerated by #2332).

⚠ Those are not the numbers this round was commissioned against, and the
difference is not drift. #2332 found the fleet has **two** live Civilization VI
seats while `publish` was copying one seat's ledger rather than merging them,
so 76 attempts were missing. Every figure above is larger than the brief's:
107 against 83, diplomatic 58 against 47, and **15 games lost while leading on
score against 4**. The sharpest line in the corrected census is that last one —
the seat has led on score and lost the game fifteen times, nearly four times
what anyone thought — and the World Congress is where the currency of the
biggest bucket is spent.

1. **Does a multi-vote World Congress purchase land yet?** #2108 shipped a
   mispricing theory — a core charging the Standard vote curve while the
   accessor reports the Online one would refuse every ask the seat had ever
   made — and pre-registered its own falsifier: *"watch the first
   `wc_ballot_verdict` with `asked = 3`; `recorded 1` kills the affordability
   theory too."* The probe shipped. **Nobody read it.**
2. **Should the contested board have a name?** `deployment-contested` exists
   only inside `--matrix`. Every single-arm round measured on it retyped the
   world by hand.
3. **What is `advanced_congress_banks_decided` worth?** It ships OFF, fires on
   13.5% of ballot decisions, and has never been gated.

## How it was measured

**The verdict, from the refusal ledger and not from a source field.** Every
`wc_ballot_verdict` row under `~/civvis-civ6-runs/control/*/events.jsonl` —
**802 rows across 28 runs** — parsed directly, plus the 2,446 `wc_vote` rows
beside them. `registered` is `recorded >= asked`
(`CivvisControlAgent.lua:13297`), and `recorded` is read back out of
`Game.GetWorldCongress():GetReview(pid)` — the host's own tally, not the mod's
opinion of what it sent. The `source` field says only which trigger fired, and
reading a `source` as a result is the discriminator this project has been
burned by before.

**The board, from the code and the recorded rounds.** `PROMOTION_PROFILES` in
`src/bin/ai_eval.rs` against the four contested rounds already in `docs/eval/`.

**The arm, on both boards, on one seed stream.**
`advanced_congress_banks_decided` against `advanced`, **60 pairs / 120 games**,
seed 36000000, run twice:

- `--profile deployment-contested` — 6 players, 74×46, 9 city-states, Online,
  250 turns, all six victories, continents/planet/poles/randomized, the four
  non-entrant chairs seated `live_target_diplomatic, live_target_culture`;
- `--profile deployment-online` — the identical world, fieldless.

Same seed prefix `36000000..=36000059`, so these are the same sixty maps. One
axis differs between the arms (`congress-banks-a-decided-vote`), so neither run
needed `--deployment-comparison`.

## What it measured

### 1. The purchase is still refused whole, and #2108's theory is dead

| asked | recorded | rows |
|---:|---:|---:|
| 1 | 1 | 663 |
| **3** (the probe) | **1** | **23** |
| 6–21 | 1 | 116 |

**139 multi-vote asks. Zero registered. The split is perfect in both
directions**, over four times the corpus #2108 was written on.

The probe is the part that settles it. Twenty-three `asked = 3` ballots across
**nine separate post-#2108 runs**, every one `recorded 1`. Three votes cost
**12 Favor on all 23** against banks of **169–427**, with `MaxVotes` 9–15 and
the verdict's own dual walk reading `host` 9–15 and `standard` 6–9 — so three
votes were inside **both** price tables on **every** probe. That is precisely
the ask no ballot had ever made when the mispricing theory was written, and it
is the ask a Standard-charging core would have honoured. **It was refused.**

Two more explanations die beside it:

- **The moment.** 14 of the 31 probe ballots were cast with
  `in_congress_segment = true`, from inside `TURNSEG_WORLDCONGRESS_*` — the
  window the shipped popup votes in. Same result.
- **The ballot.** `option_asked == option_recorded` on **82.8%** of one-vote
  rows and **73.4%** of multi-vote rows. The ballot registers and flips its
  option at essentially the same rate either way; only the **count** is
  clamped.

And the parameter path is not the difference. Firaxis' own `OnAccept` in the
installed game — `…/Expansion2/UI/Additions/WorldCongressPopup.lua:2239-2270` —
sends `PARAM_RESOLUTION_TYPE`, `PARAM_WORLD_CONGRESS_VOTES`,
`PARAM_RESOLUTION_OPTION`, `PARAM_RESOLUTION_SELECTION` through
`WORLD_CONGRESS_RESOLUTION_VOTE` and then `WORLD_CONGRESS_SUBMIT_TURN`. The mod
sends the same four parameters through the same operation and the same submit
(`CivvisControlAgent.lua:14040-14093`).

**Verdict: still refused whole. `PARAM_WORLD_CONGRESS_VOTES > 1` is not
honoured through `UI.RequestPlayerOperation` for this seat, and every
explanation the mod controls is now eliminated with data rather than argued.**

### 2. And the ledger has been reporting a spend that never happened

`wc_vote.spent` is the mod's **model** of the stake: it walks the host's cost
table and adds the charge for every vote it *asked* for. The host charges for
the votes it *records*, and the first vote on any ballot is free.

**620 `wc_vote` rows report a spend above zero, totalling 333,840 Favor.** For
69 of those ballots a verdict was later readable: **20,520 Favor reported
spent, 884 votes asked, 205 recorded — and not one multi-vote ask registered.**
Every recorded vote was a first vote, at `costs[0] = 0`.

⚠ This is the shape `AGENTS.md` names as *"a setting you send is read back from
wherever it lands"*, and it is why the 961-resolution drought took twenty-nine
runs to see: the instrument that should have shouted was quietly agreeing with
the plan.

### 3. The hand-typed contested board was never the contested board

Three rounds in `docs/eval/` measured congress arms on
`--field live_target_diplomatic,live_target_culture`, and each records its world
as **`pangaea`/`flat`/fixed civilizations**:
`2026-08-18-the-screen-can-seat-the-rivals-firaxis-actually-plays`,
`2026-08-18-the-promotion-matrix-can-see-the-lanes-the-front-line-loses-`, and
`2026-08-18-the-vote-weight-and-the-ballot-agree-on-who-the-counter-oppo`
(which reuses the second's world explicitly: *"the identical run … same maps,
same field"*).

`deployment-contested`, the profile the promotion gate actually runs, has been
**`continents`/`planet`/`poles`/randomized** since #658 —
`matrix_child_args` hard-coded `--map`, `--shape`, `--poles` and
`--randomize-civs` *outside* the profile struct, where nothing could compare
them to anything. Eleven world flags typed by hand, agreeing with the gate on
eight.

⚠ **And a fourth round on the same field got all eleven right.**
`2026-08-19-the-suzerainty-prize-was-retired-on-a-board-with-diplomacy-s`
prints its command line in full and it is the gate's world exactly. That is the
finding, not an exoneration: whether a hand-typed contested round was on the
contested board was a property of the person typing, and nothing in the
repository could tell the two apart afterwards. There was no second expansion
of the profile for a test to disagree with.

### 4. The arm, on both boards

The arm never changed a game on the contested board, and changed two of sixty
on the fieldless one. **Both runs are `INCONCLUSIVE`, and one of them is not
even a null.**

| | contested | fieldless |
|---|---|---|
| paired-map score | **50.0%** (95% betting CI 44.4%..55.6%) | **50.0%** (44.4%..55.6%) |
| Elo-equivalent | **+0** (CI −39..+39) | **+0** (−39..+39) |
| maps that broke on **wins** | **0** of 60 | **0** of 60 |
| maps that broke on **terminal score** | **0** of 60 | **2** of 60 |
| gate | `INCONCLUSIVE` | `INCONCLUSIVE` |
| average game length | 209.1 turns | 212.9 turns |

On the contested board the harness printed its own non-measurement warning:

> ⚠ nothing differed: all 60 maps were neutral on wins AND on terminal score,
> so `advanced_congress_banks_decided` and `advanced` played the same games.
> The verdict above is not evidence about the treatment — it did not fire on
> this profile.

⚠ **Read that as the resolution cost of the contested profile, not as a
property of the arm.** Fieldless the entrants hold all six chairs; contested
they hold two. There are one-third as many treated seats casting ballots, so
the board that finally produces the lane also has the fewest seats able to act
in it. The same arm on the same sixty maps fired twice fieldless and not at all
contested.

**And the arm is a small one wherever it runs.** 2 of 60 maps is the whole of
its measured footprint across 240 games. Firing on 13.5% of *ballot decisions*
(#2091's frequency count) is not the same as changing a *game*: at ~1.4 free
Diplomatic Victory Points per seat per game against the 20 a Diplomatic victory
needs, and on a board where the entrants win no diplomatic games at all, there
is no channel through which it could have moved this score. **Nothing here says
it is worthless; it says whole-game paired scoring at 60 pairs cannot see it,
and a longer run of the same design would buy a tighter interval around a
treatment that did not act.**

### 5. ★★★★★ But the board itself is the result

The victory mix is where the two runs stop agreeing, and it is decisive.

| | religious | culture | diplomatic | science | score |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| **fieldless** (`deployment-online`), 120 games | 53 | 6 | **0** | 7 | 54 |
| **contested** (`deployment-contested`), same 60 maps | 36 | **56** | **8** | 2 | 18 |

Same shape, same seeds, same arm. The field turns **diplomatic 0 into 8 of 120**
(Fisher exact **p = 0.0069**) and **culture 6 into 56** (**p = 2.8e-14**). The
lane that has taken 58 live games and that no promotion decision in this
repository's history could see is on the board.

⚠ The Fisher tests above treat the two runs as independent samples. They share
their sixty map seeds but not their games — four of six seats are different
agents — so the terrain is common and the play is not. That correlation is a
reason to read the *sizes* rather than the exact p-values; at 2.8e-14 and
0.0069 no plausible correlation closes the culture gap, and the diplomatic one
is already reported as weak below.

**And the gate's own world produces it four times better than the board every
recorded contested round was measured on.** Against #2042's contested reading
(60 pairs / 120 games, same field, same six-player Online 74×46/9CS shape, but
`pangaea`/`flat`/fixed):

| contested field, 120 games | religious | culture | diplomatic |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| `pangaea`/`flat`/fixed (#2042, seed 33000000) | 90 | 24 (20%) | **2 (1.7%)** |
| `continents`/`planet`/`poles`/randomized (here, seed 36000000) | 36 | **56 (47%)** | **8 (6.7%)** |

Culture: **p = 1.8e-05**. Religious: **p = 3.2e-12**. Diplomatic 2 → 8 is
**p = 0.10** — suggestive and not significant on its own, and it should not be
quoted as if it were; what is not in doubt is that the map script moves this
distribution hard, and that the direction is toward the lanes the front line
loses to.

⚠ **This retracts a standing conclusion.** #2042 measured that adding a third
diplomacy seat did not add diplomatic victories and concluded *"the diplomatic
yield is not a seat-count problem, so the obvious lever does not exist —
CIVVIS's own diplomacy lane rarely completes at all."* That was measured on
`pangaea`. On the board the promotion gate actually runs, the same two-seat
field yields four times as many diplomatic victories. **The lever it looked for
may exist; it was looking on the wrong map.**

### 6. ⚠⚠ And the contested board cannot price a denial treatment through its
win column

The harness said this itself, unprompted, and it is the most important line
either run printed:

> ⚠ produced but not resolvable here (interval half-width 5.6 points): culture
> decided 56 of 120 games but an entrant won 0 of them, so it can move the
> paired score by at most 0.0 points; diplomatic decided 8 of 120 games but an
> entrant won 0 of them, so it can move the paired score by at most 0.0 points

The entrants won **25 of 120 games each (20.8%)**; the field took the other
58%, and **every single culture and diplomatic victory in 120 games went to a
field seat.** A game a field seat wins is a draw for the pair by construction
(#2038), so a treatment whose effect is expressed *by winning* one of those
lanes is bounded at **zero** points of paired movement — not underpowered,
bounded. More pairs cannot fix that.

⚠ Denial is not quite that case, and the round must not overstate it. A denial
treatment's channel is indirect: prevent the field's culture win, the game runs
to the clock, an entrant takes it on score or religion — and entrants did win
48 of the 54 religious-or-score games, so that channel is open. What the bound
establishes is narrower and still sharp: **the contested profile's win column
reports denial only through a second-order conversion, and reports a lane win
not at all.** Anything reading the first column as "did the denial work" is
reading the wrong number.

## What was decided

**1. The purchase verdict is recorded, and the mod is not changed to chase
it.** `CivvisCongressVoteBudget`'s dual-table cap is kept. Its own paragraph
already said why that is free: when the theory is wrong the cap asks fewer
votes on a ballot that registers one either way, so removing it changes no
outcome and would churn a file no headless test can exercise. What is removed
is the impression that the question is open — the ★★★★ block now carries the
probe's answer, with the corpus behind it.

⚠ **What remains is host-side and unreachable from this file.** Every
explanation the mod controls is eliminated: parameters, operation, submit,
trigger, moment, option, budget, cap, and repeat-send. The run that would
settle the rest is one live game in which a person clicks two votes onto a
resolution through the real popup on the same seat and turn that the agent
ballot asks for two, and `wc_outcome` is read for both. That needs the live
harness, which has been under an operator halt since 2026-08-19.
**Not started, and it should not be started to answer this.**

**2. The ballot ledger reads the bank back.** `wc_vote` now carries
`favor_after` beside the `favor_before` it already read, and `favor_asked`
beside `spent`, so a row says on its own face whether the Favor it claims to
have staked ever moved. **No game behaviour changes**: nothing about the ask,
the budget, the target or the option is touched, and the pinned return shape
(`return cast, spent, nil, leader, …`) is unchanged. ⚠ A player operation is
queued rather than applied inline, so `favor_after` is a lower bound on what
was taken and `wc_ballot_verdict.favor_now` at the next review remains the
settled figure. Both together are strictly more than a forecast alone.

**3. `--profile <name>` ships, and the three recorded profiles are its
vocabulary.** `compact-standard`, `deployment-online`, `deployment-contested`.
One expansion — `profile_axis_args` — now builds both the matrix's children and
a named single-arm run, and `map`/`shape`/`poles`/`randomize_civs` moved out of
`matrix_child_args` into the profile struct so a profile names its whole world
instead of eight-elevenths of it. Like `--matrix`, `--profile` **refuses** any
of the thirteen world axes beside it rather than letting the explicit flag win;
`--pairs`, `--jobs`, `--seed`, `--confirm` and `--difficulty` stay free. Every
run prints the profile it resolved, or `ad hoc (no --profile)`.

The test is the point of the change, not the flag: for every profile, the named
expansion and the matrix child must agree on all eleven axes. Written against
the discrepancy above, it fails on any future edit to one expansion and not the
other.

⚠ **This does not retroactively repair the three drifted rounds.** They
measured what they measured, on `pangaea`/`flat`/fixed, and §5 now says what
that cost them. What changes is that a round from here can cite a name, and the
name is checked.

**4. `advanced_congress_banks_decided` stays OFF, and this round does not gate
it.** `INCONCLUSIVE` on both boards, +0 Elo (−39..+39), and on the contested
board it did not fire at all. That is a null on the fieldless board and a
non-measurement on the contested one, and the difference matters: the harness's
own warning says the second is not evidence about the treatment. **A longer run
of this design is explicitly not the next step** — the repository has now
recorded three times that buying more pairs of a treatment which never fires
buys nothing.

**5. The contested profile is shown to work and shown to have a hole, and both
are recorded.** It produces the lanes (diplomatic 0 → 8, culture 6 → 56 on the
same sixty maps) and it cannot report a lane victory through its win column,
because every one of them went to a field seat and a field seat's win is a
draw. Nothing is changed about the scoring here — #2038 chose seat-indexed
scoring deliberately and changing it is a separate decision with its own
evidence — but a round that reads the contested win column as "did denial work"
is reading the wrong number, and now there is a place that says so.

**5. Two denial treatments could not be priced here, and one of them cannot be
priced by any native run that exists.**

- **`deny-while-targeted` is inert on every arm that carries it.** The gate is
  `actionable_victory_denial_with_culture_pressures`
  (`src/ai/advanced/victory_heuristics.rs:150-153`): it returns early only when
  `targeted`, and `targeted` is `self.active_victory_target(g).is_some()`,
  which reads `self.victory_target` — set by `AdvancedAi::targeting` and by
  nothing else (`src/ai/advanced.rs:5766`). `enable_live_bridge` says so
  itself where it turns the flag on: *"the live seat plays under an assigned
  lane (`--victory science`), and `victory_denial` stands down entirely for a
  targeted seat"*. The adaptive `live` agent has no assigned lane, so
  `targeted` is false and the branch never runs — and the only withholding arm
  that exists, `live_without_deny_while_targeted`, is that same adaptive `live`
  minus the tag. **The two arms are behaviourally one agent**, and a run of
  them would produce the harness's own *"nothing differed"* warning rather
  than a null.

  Pricing it natively needs an arm seated on a lane-assigned agent —
  `live_target_diplomatic` minus `deny-while-targeted` — which does not exist.
  That is three coordinated entries in `src/elo.rs` (`EVAL_ONLY_AIS`, an
  `ArmKind`, a `build_arm` case) plus a manifest regeneration, in a file two
  open pull requests claim today. **Recorded as the next round's
  prerequisite**, not attempted inside this one.

- **`stock-denial-lead-time` is measurable and was not measured, for budget.**
  Unlike the above it has a path that does not require an assigned lane:
  `culture_denial_actionable` (`src/ai/advanced.rs:8087`) is consulted on every
  candidate rival regardless of `targeted`, so `live_without_stock_denial_lead_time`
  against `live` is a real contrast. It costs what any live-bundle pair costs,
  which is the expensive kind: the bundle carries `joint-tactics`, measured at
  2.52× the whole batch.

- **`congress-counter-votes` is no longer unmeasured.** #2043 repaired the arm
  so it can fire and re-read it at 60 pairs on the contested field: 50.0%
  (44.4%..55.6%), Elo +0 (−39..+39), `INCONCLUSIVE`, firing on 3 of 60 maps.
  ⚠ On the hand-typed `pangaea`/`flat` board, per §3.

- **`lane-congress-favor` is no longer unmeasured either.** It is a gene, not
  an evaluator arm, and `docs/VICTORY_GENES.md` §8.5 carries it at 984 pairs:
  +1.4 points (17.4% against 16.0%), z +1.42.

**7. What a properly-powered follow-up costs.** Measured on this machine:
**~59 CPU-seconds per game** at the contested shape (from a two-game pilot on
an unloaded box), so **60 pairs / 120 games ≈ 2.0 CPU-hours**; the two runs
here were ~4 CPU-hours together and took about 50 minutes of wall clock at
`--jobs 4` each on a box averaging load 65.

- Halving the ±39 Elo interval needs **4× the maps: 240 pairs / 480 games
  ≈ 7.9 CPU-hours** per arm per board. For an arm that broke 2 of 60 maps that
  buys a tighter interval around a treatment that barely acts, which is the
  wrong purchase.
- The purchase worth making instead is **an arm that can fire**: a lane-pinned
  withholding control (`live_target_diplomatic` minus `deny-while-targeted`)
  costs the same 2.0 CPU-hours to read at 60 pairs and would be the first
  measurement of that treatment that is not structurally a no-op.
- And the cheapest useful run of all is a **replication of §5 on a disjoint
  seed prefix** — 60 pairs fieldless plus 60 contested at seed 37000000,
  ≈4 CPU-hours — because §5's diplomatic 2 → 8 across map scripts is `p = 0.10`
  and is the one number here that a second sitting could overturn.
