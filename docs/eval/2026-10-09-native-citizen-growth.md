## Native release regression and correction

Panamá (city 524295) exposed a release defect in the first attempt. At turn 104, frame 0, it had population 4, food stock 0.210938, housing 5 and 10 worked Food, with surplus 2. Releasing Food at that point changed the native allocation to 7 worked Food and surplus -1 in frame 1. At turn 105 it had population 3 and food stock 15, then lost one Food per turn. Its queue remained Walls and no Settler existed in these states. The preference release was verified, but its new allocation caused starvation. Treating a changed preference alone as a successful release was insufficient.

The corrected bridge keeps release ownership until the host reports both an unfavored Food preference and a finite, nonnegative Food surplus. Negative or unknown surplus requests restoration of the owned Food preference. The central `CivvisQueue.requestEndTurn` guard holds ordinary and forced submissions while release or restoration remains pending; a queued request alone cannot release that hold. A restored preference stays managed, and another release is refused until native population, housing or favored Food surplus changes. This preserves external preferences and does not add a second allocator. Getter failures use an unknown value rather than interpreting the export's -1 fallback as a trustworthy quote.

The Lua regression exercises the actual end-turn function, delayed native allocation, negative and unknown surplus, restoration failure/retry, repeated unsafe requests, changed housing, safe release and changed city ownership. The original six Rust decision/readback tests remain applicable. The correction changes Lua control behavior, not Rust mechanics or game settings.
