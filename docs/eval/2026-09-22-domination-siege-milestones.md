# Preserve a progressing Domination siege before fatigue-only peace

Native083707 continuation offered peace to the Maori on turn 218 because the war had stalled, despite military power 1261 versus 569 and a steadily advancing siege of the named objective, Whaingaroa. Wall damage rose from 151/400 on turn 209 to 312/400 on turn 218, then reached 336/400 on turn 219 before peace allowed repairs. The default fatigue clock records own-city growth; neither optional siege-progress rule was in this native genome.

For an explicit Domination target, track newly crossed quarters of combined city and wall damage while a healthy land force is within three tiles. Fix the full-defense scale at first observation, so stronger wall capacity cannot manufacture progress. Consume each threshold once per continuous war. Initial wounds, rehealing, repeated damage and gaps in observations do not renew credit. A threshold less than twelve turns old suppresses only fatigue for the named Conquest objective while the army remains present and the plan reports no threatened home city. Outmatched, recovery, second-front and other peace reasons remain independent. Outgoing offers and incoming peace valuation use the same signal.

The history is keyed by owner and map position. Native mirrors renumber internal city IDs between snapshots: diagnostics recorded Whaingaroa as IDs 86/85 during turn 217 and 88 on turn 218. An ID-keyed prototype lost the history or attached it to a different city and changed neither replay. The stable-key regression rebuilds cities under different IDs and reproduces that failure before the correction. Peace, disappearance or ownership change clears the corresponding history.
