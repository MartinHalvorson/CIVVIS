# Strike forecast arrival reader

The fixed Emperor native attempt `civvis-20261009T092113Z` lost to Germany's Science victory at turn 195. Its late frames took 54–129 seconds to decide. A five-second sample of an unchanged recorded-control process gathered 450 samples: 330 were in `unit_reply_with_forecast`, 304 in `DangerField::second_turn`, and 217 in `Game::approach_reach`. This sample locates a cost; it does not establish an end-to-end speedup or a win-rate gain. The historical control is not exact across the whole native game and is not credited as a native decision comparison.

`strike_reach_of` asks `approach_reach` for destinations and movement remaining, discarding every constructed path. The proposed `approach_arrivals` uses the same movement flood, retained-movement arrival type, passability and stopping gates. It omits parent tracking and path materialization and returns a contiguous vector. The strike reader sorts and deduplicates its resulting target set, so arrival discovery order is not an exposed decision order. Existing path callers keep the original path reader.

The contract tests compare every destination and the exact floating-point movement bits against the existing engine reader across partial movement, rough ground, roads, hostile zones of control, a friendly column and naval movement. They separately require that a friendly unit may be crossed but may not be an endpoint, preserve missing/spent-unit empty readings, and check that queries do not mutate units. A manual alternating-order kernel benchmark measures both readers without a timing assertion on the shared host. Validation and performance measurements are pending at this checkpoint.

The current native queue also contains a separate external change that disarms `unit-preservation`; that policy change is preserved and cannot be credited to this optimization. The fixed verification challenge remains unchanged. Native wins, including all non-winning retirements in the denominator, remain the success criterion for decision changes. This optimization must first preserve decisions and demonstrate measured runtime benefit.
