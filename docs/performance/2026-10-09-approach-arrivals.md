# Strike forecast arrival reader

The fixed Emperor native attempt `civvis-20261009T092113Z` lost to Germany's Science victory at turn 195. Its late frames took 54–129 seconds to decide. A five-second sample of an unchanged recorded-control process gathered 450 samples: 330 were in `unit_reply_with_forecast`, 304 in `DangerField::second_turn`, and 217 in `Game::approach_reach`. This sample locates a cost; it does not establish an end-to-end speedup or a win-rate gain. The historical control is not exact across the whole native game and is not credited as a native decision comparison.

`strike_reach_of` asks `approach_reach` for destinations and movement remaining, discarding every constructed path. The proposed `approach_arrivals` uses the same movement flood, retained-movement arrival type, passability and stopping gates. It omits parent tracking and path materialization and returns a contiguous vector. The strike reader sorts and deduplicates its resulting target set, so arrival discovery order is not an exposed decision order. Existing path callers keep the original path reader.

The contract tests compare every destination and the exact floating-point movement bits against the existing engine reader across partial movement, rough ground, roads, hostile zones of control, a friendly column and naval movement. They separately require that a friendly unit may be crossed but may not be an endpoint, preserve missing/spent-unit empty readings, and check that queries do not mutate units. A manual alternating-order kernel benchmark measures both readers without a timing assertion on the shared host. The native contract run passes all three cases, including 60 movement scenarios; the manual benchmark remains ignored in the correctness run. Initial compile attempts exposed fixture errors in storage access and the feature name, which were corrected; no movement-bit mismatch was reported. The fixture now uses a named type for its unit-fact tuple to satisfy Clippy. The first local full-suite attempt compiled an earlier, invalid fixture and failed. The complete corrected governed run, `cargo test --profile ci --locked -- --test-threads=2`, passes 4,464 library, 14 main, 247 controller, 17 integration and 4 viewer tests; 50 existing library cases, the manual benchmark and 4 existing documentation cases remain ignored.

The release kernel measurement completed six alternating-order pairs of 300 queries per reader on one road-heavy cavalry fixture. Both readers returned 26,400 destinations in each sample. Median durations were 7,547,499.5 ns for the path reader and 4,998,812.5 ns for the arrival reader; the median paired change was −34.14%. This measures the movement-query kernel on that fixture, not whole-game or native-frame latency. The integrated native candidate also passes 21 movement-rule tests, 64 battle-planner tests, 12 unit-preservation tests and all 272 controller tests, and both release binaries build.

The initial public CI cost comparison completed the same 600 turns in both arms over five paired seeds. It measured a median 1.80% reduction in time per completed turn, an IQR of 2.06 percentage points over −3.96% to −0.41%, and resolution of approximately ±1.36%. This supports a small simulator timing benefit on that workload; it does not measure native frame latency or establish a win-rate change. Runtime source in that comparison is unchanged by the subsequent fixture-only corrections.

Two later CI comparisons also completed the same 600 turns per arm over the same five paired seeds. Their median changes were −2.06% and −2.81%. The former had an IQR of 0.39 percentage points and resolution of ±0.27%; the latter was noisier, with an IQR of 5.74 percentage points over −5.91% to +1.75% and resolution of ±3.81%. These are repeated measurements on the tested workload, not fifteen independent maps or a precise native speedup.
